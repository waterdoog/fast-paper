\section{Scoring rules fixed before key authoring}
\label{app:scoring}
Three rules are fixed before any key is written. The schema---field names, types, and
the full enumeration vocabulary---is given to the agent, so that only values are unknown
and we are not measuring whether a model guesses our vocabulary. Normalization is
specified in advance: numbers parsed and compared exactly, dates ISO-8601, strings
casefolded and trimmed, enumerations restricted to the supplied list, lists compared as
sets. Evidence is matched on values rather than on internal identifiers, which agents
never see.

\section{Controls and preregistration}
\label{app:controls}
One model across all agents and all cells; one runtime; one kernel. Identical task
prompts, deliverable schemas, scoring functions, token budgets, and seed distributions.
Rate limits and loop bounds are logged per episode rather than absorbed into results.
Rubrics, schemas, normalization rules and the scorer signature are frozen before any
run; the mapping from each hypothesis to the figure that tests it is fixed in advance
and reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:map}.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{app:threats}
A null on the textual group could be a prompt-engineering artifact rather than a
property of the runtime: a stronger seed, placed differently in context, might move
behavior. We mitigate by seeding at three intensities and reporting the dose-response,
and by including the \dimname{relationship origin} condition, which is the weakest
possible seed and therefore the cleanest test of whether framing alone does anything. A
null could also be a ceiling effect if the tasks are too easy to differentiate
configurations; \metric{realization} is reported partly to detect this.

\section{Relay: a bounded roster given a second hop}
\label{app:relay}
A bounded network here addresses twenty holders and no others, which is a truncated
directory rather than a private network: information reaches a closed group through the
people in it, one hop further out. We therefore let a roster member forward one question
to a contact of its own, minted under its own authority rather than derived from the
requester's, and re-ran. \textbf{The gap did not close.} Across 64 episodes at sixteen per
cell relay moves the bounded arm by $+0.042$ (CI $[-0.137, 0.208]$), and the
difference-in-differences against the open arm --- whose configuration is identical under
both settings and which serves as the negative control, moving $-0.063$ --- is $+0.105$
(CI $[-0.105, 0.322]$).

Two details survive it. The same contrast read $+0.255$ at six episodes per cell with an
interval excluding zero and decayed monotonically with $n$. And excluding episodes that
produced no artifact, the bounded arm scores $0.734$ with relay against $0.554$ without:
relay helps substantially \emph{when it completes} and costs enough exploration budget
that seven episodes never reached a build --- five under relay and two under a high
first-contact cost without it, so the failure belongs to any mechanism that consumes extra
turns, and exposes a harness that answers budget exhaustion by refusing tools rather than
by forcing a build.
