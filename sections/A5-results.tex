\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers below come from 144 episodes on one kernel, one runtime, and one
model: 72 for the $2\times2$ over edge formation and edge semantics, 72 for the
attribute axis. Bootstrap intervals are percentile over episodes, resampled
within cell.

\subsection{Edge formation dominates (H1)}

Across seven tests---four accuracy metrics and three cost metrics, over the cold,
rework and warm beats---$\Delta_\text{formation}$ clears its interval in six.
Chance at $\alpha = 0.05$ would produce $0.4$. The sign is stable and the
magnitude is largest where the task most needs outside knowledge:
at a high external fraction it reaches $0.352$, against $0.112$ when most
holders sit inside the roster.

It is also cheaper. Opening the directory saves $17.1$k tokens per episode
(CI excludes 0) while raising accuracy, because a requester that can address the
right holder stops paying for the search around it.

\subsection{Edge semantics: a small gain at a real price (H3)}

$\Delta_\text{semantics}$ clears in two of the same seven tests, against a
chance floor of $0.4$. The sign is positive in all three beats
($0.080$, $0.028$, $0.098$) but only the warm beat's interval excludes zero, so
we report it as small and underpowered rather than absent.

The cost side is unambiguous, and it inverts the ranking. Reading a
counterpart's store costs $11.0$k tokens per episode (CI excludes 0). Per token
spent, agent mediation is ahead in both arms:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
& sandboxed & readable & ratio \\
\midrule
open    & 0.177 & 0.157 & $1.13\times$ \\
bounded & 0.094 & 0.087 & $1.08\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent
$F_1$ per 10k tokens. Exposing state buys a little accuracy; delegating to an
agent that already holds the context buys more of it per unit of context spent.
This is the tradeoff between moving data to the computation and moving the
computation to the data, measured on an agent network.

\subsection{Edge attributes are inert (H2)}

The attribute axis asserts five relational properties in context---prior trust,
attribution, a two-year colleague framing, a stranger framing, and a
length-matched placebo---with the network configuration held fixed.

\midrule

Two results matter here. First, the placebo settles the alternative explanation:
two lines of neutral text, matched for length and position, cost $+0.005$ with an
interval spanning zero. Whatever the relational seeds do, they do not do it by
occupying tokens.

Second, nothing survives. Eight contrasts across seven metrics is 56 tests per
beat, so chance alone yields about $2.8$; two cleared, and they cleared \emph{in
opposite directions in different beats of the same episodes}. The narration
instrument---the cleanest of the set, differing in one sentence and nothing
else---is flat at $0.029$. The two assertions that deployed reputation systems
actually implement, prior trust and attribution, are the flattest of all.
