\section{Conclusion}

Agent networks encode relationships in three places, and comparisons of open against
closed networks move all three at once. Separating them on one kernel, one runtime and
one model, a regime contrast of $0.313$ turns out to be three quarters a question of
which edges may be formed, one quarter a question of what an edge carries, and nothing
at all a question of what is asserted about it. A length-matched placebo rules out the
obvious alternative, and the two properties deployed reputation systems actually
carry---prior trust and attribution---are the flattest measurements we have.

The formation advantage is narrower than it looks. It is a reach advantage: it shrinks
by two thirds when the roster already holds what a task needs, and reverses on the beat
that requires returning to a counterpart already used. It is also measured in a network
where forming an edge is free, which is the condition under which open discovery is
worth most and the assumption a follow-up should relax first.

For an agentic web the implication is narrow and, we think, useful. Changing what a
network permits changes what the collective does. Describing the relationship does not
--- not at the layer where descriptions currently live.
