\section{Conclusion}

Public and private agent networks are usually distinguished by a vocabulary of
relationships---trust, shared goals, accountability, tenure---and implemented by a
vocabulary of mechanisms---who is addressable, what may be read, what persists. We
separated the two, ran the mechanisms as a factorial on one kernel, and seeded the
relational vocabulary as context to see whether a runtime acts on it.

The framing we recommend is not that one regime is better. It is that a regime is a
configuration, that only part of the configuration is enforced, and that the enforced
part is where the behavior is. Open discovery widens what a task can reach; a bounded,
readable, persistent relationship is what lets a task be finished and revised. Between
them sits a boundary that costs something to cross, and the question worth asking of
any agent network is not whether it is open or closed but which of its stated
relationships the runtime can actually be made to honour.
