Agent networks are being built to look like Twitter or like WhatsApp, and the two are
compared as though ``public versus private'' were a variable. It is not. An edge is a
triple---who may form it, what it carries once formed, what is merely asserted about
it---and the regimes are two bundles that differ on all three at once. Comparing them
measures a bundle swap, and which number it returns is set by a pairing nobody documents:
the two diagonals of the same factorial give $0.181$ and $0.314$ on the same episodes. We
give an instrument that sets the three axes independently---one model, one runtime, one
coordination kernel, conditions expressed as capability grants---and a metric set that
prices each axis in accuracy, tokens and disclosure rather than in accuracy alone. Across
582 episodes of hidden-profile coordination scored against forced-value answer keys, the
factorial recovers what neither diagonal isolates: $0.248$, $0.066$ and $0$ on requirement
$F_1$. A length-matched placebo moves the score as much as a trust assertion does, and the
formation effect is reach, vanishing when the roster already holds what a task needs. On
every axis we measured the bounded arm is weakly dominated---it never scores higher and
always costs $\sim$19k more tokens a task, including where it merely ties---and two of its
results were not designed. It is shown a quarter as much misinformation as an open directory
and writes more of it into the deliverable, fabricating values 58 times where the open arms
fabricated once: filtering out bad claims also removes the second source that catches them.
And a single brokered hop recovers $+0.140$ of the gap while every assertion of trust
remains flat, separating an introduction, a formation intervention, from an endorsement, an
attribute. If a private agent network is worth building, the reason is not information
quality, and our measurements do not contain it.
