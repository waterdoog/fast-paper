Agent networks are being built to look like Twitter or like WhatsApp, and the two are
compared as though ``public versus private'' were a variable. It is not. An edge is a
triple---who may form it, what it carries once formed, what is merely asserted about
it---and the two regimes are not two values of one dial but two \emph{bundles} that differ
on all three at once: lateral breadth, sandboxed exchange and platform-computed scores
against longitudinal depth, openable state and relationship-carried history. Comparing
them measures a bundle swap and reports one number that cannot be attributed to any
component. We give an instrument that sets the
three independently---one model, one runtime, one coordination kernel, conditions
expressed as capability grants---and a metric set that prices each axis in accuracy,
tokens and disclosure rather than in accuracy alone. Across 582 episodes of
hidden-profile coordination scored against forced-value answer keys, the three axes
weigh roughly $0.232$, $0.080$ and $0$ on requirement $F_1$: a length-matched placebo
moves the score as much as a trust assertion does, and the formation effect is reach,
vanishing when the roster already holds what a task needs. Two results were not
designed. A bounded roster is shown a quarter as much misinformation as an open
directory and writes more of it into the deliverable, fabricating values 58 times where
the open arms fabricated once---filtering out bad claims also removes the second source
that catches them. And a single brokered hop recovers $+0.140$ of the gap while every
assertion of trust remains flat, which separates an introduction, a formation
intervention, from an endorsement, an attribute. The weights are measured on one task
family; whether they are constants of the framework or of the task is the experiment the
framework now specifies.
