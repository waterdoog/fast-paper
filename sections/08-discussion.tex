\section{Discussion and limitations}

\paragraph{If architecture dominates.}
The immediate implication is for where design effort goes. Agent cards are acquiring
reputation fields, alignment declarations and relationship summaries on the assumption
that a model will act on them. If the textual group is inert, that surface is
decoration, and the same effort spent making the runtime enforce something---a grant
that expires, a namespace that persists, a directory that is actually bounded---buys
behavior change that text does not. It would also mean that published comparisons of
open against closed agent networks are, to a first approximation, reporting the effect
of membership.

\paragraph{If the textual group is not inert.}
Then the paper reports elasticities rather than a null, and those are directly usable:
how much verification a trust assertion removes, how much error an accountability
assertion prevents, whether framing alone shifts a stopping rule. We would then expect
follow-up work on which assertions survive an adversarial counterpart, since a
relational claim that changes behavior is also a relational claim worth forging.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Seeded relational properties are assertions, not relationships}
(Section~\ref{sec:seeding}); the study measures runtime sensitivity to asserted
context and nothing stronger. \emph{The cold-phase crossing is designed}, and only its
location, cost and realization are measured. \emph{Forced-value deliverables cannot
capture whether an artifact is well argued}, which is the price of removing the judge
from the primary metrics; the three judged metrics recover part of it under stated
conditions and with agreement reported. \emph{The study runs on a reference
implementation of the coordination kernel, not on a production deployment}: the
architectural knobs and the capability model are drawn from deployed source, but the
runs are not deployment traces, and we describe the work as measured on a reference
kernel rather than as a deployment study. Finally, \emph{one model family carries the
main sweep}; a replication on a second family is reported, but relational sensitivity
is plausibly model-specific and we do not claim otherwise.

\paragraph{Threats to validity we can name in advance.}
A null on the textual group could be a prompt-engineering artifact rather than a
property of the runtime: a stronger seed, placed differently in context, might move
behavior. We mitigate by seeding at three intensities and reporting the dose-response,
and by including the \dimname{relationship origin} condition, which is the weakest
possible seed and therefore the cleanest test of whether framing alone does anything.
A null could also be a ceiling effect if the tasks are too easy to differentiate
configurations; \metric{realization} is reported partly to detect this.
