\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Ties, teams, and open innovation.}
The intuition that open networks supply variety and bounded ones supply follow-through
descends from work on weak ties and social capital \cite{granovetter1973}, on
exploration and exploitation in organizational learning \cite{march1991}, on
transactive memory and team familiarity \cite{wegner1987}, and on open versus closed
innovation \cite{chesbrough2003}. That literature studies humans, where the relational
properties in our textual group are real rather than asserted. We take its dimensions
and ask a question it cannot: whether a model acts on them when they arrive as text.

\paragraph{Hidden profiles.}
Our task construction is the hidden-profile paradigm \cite{stasser1985}: information
required for the correct decision is distributed so that no participant can reach it
alone. We adopt it because it supplies exact ground truth and because it makes
pooling, rather than retrieval, the object of measurement.

\paragraph{Agent protocols and networks.}
Agent-to-agent task protocols expose a capability card and accept tasks while
deliberately keeping a counterpart opaque \cite{a2a2025}; broadcast networks for
agents route published needs and capabilities among strangers while stating that a
participant's private information does not enter the network \cite{eigenflux};
surveys of the emerging protocol landscape catalogue the design space
\cite{agentprotocols2025,coral2025}. These systems encode exactly the split we study,
between exposing an interface and exposing a store, but to our knowledge none has been
evaluated for which side of the split changes agent behavior.

\paragraph{Capabilities and delegation.}
The authority model is object-capability discipline \cite{miller2006}: authority is
held rather than named, delegation may only attenuate, and a message conveys intent
rather than permission. We rely on it so that a condition is a set of grants a kernel
checks, not a convention an agent could ignore.

\paragraph{LLM judges.}
Where an answer key cannot reach, we use pairwise blind comparison \cite{zheng2023},
with a cross-family judge because evaluators favour their own generations
\cite{panickssery2024}, and we report agreement rather than assuming it.
