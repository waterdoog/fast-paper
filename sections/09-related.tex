\section{Related work}

Agent-to-agent task protocols publish a capability card and accept tasks while keeping a
counterpart opaque \citep{a2a2025}; broadcast networks route published needs among
strangers \citep{eigenflux}. Both fix a point on our formation and semantics axes without
treating either as a variable. Which links form, and at what price, is strategic network
formation \citep{jackson1996}; our formation axis is that question with the cost set near
zero, and weak ties are its classical statement \citep{granovetter1973}. What an edge
\emph{carries} has no comparable body of work, and we take the distinction from deployed
protocol design; the underlying discipline is object-capability
\citep{miller2006}. Attributes on an edge are a standard attributed graph, but treating
them as a control surface and measuring whether they work is, to our knowledge, new. Our
tasks are the hidden-profile paradigm \citep{stasser1985}, and no primary metric uses a
judge, which avoids the self-preference confound \citep{panickssery2024}.

\section{Related work}

Agent-to-agent task protocols publish a capability card and accept tasks while
deliberately keeping a counterpart opaque \citep{a2a2025}; broadcast networks route
published needs among strangers while stating that private information does not enter the
network \citep{eigenflux}; surveys catalogue the wider design space
\citep{agentprotocols2025,coral2025}. These encode exactly the split we intervene on ---
whether an edge carries a delegated task or direct access to state --- and capability
discovery is the first thrust of current agentic-web research, but to our knowledge
neither has been evaluated for which side of it changes collective behavior. Which links form, and at what
cost, is the subject of strategic network formation \citep{jackson1996}; our formation axis
is that question with the cost set to zero, which is the assumption a follow-up should
relax first. The intuition that open networks supply variety and bounded ones supply
follow-through descends from weak ties \citep{granovetter1973}, exploration and exploitation
\citep{march1991}, transactive memory \citep{wegner1987}, and open innovation
\citep{chesbrough2003}, all studying humans, where the properties in our attribute group
are real rather than asserted. Our tasks are the hidden-profile paradigm
\citep{stasser1985}, which supplies exact ground truth and makes pooling rather than
retrieval the object of measurement; the authority model is object-capability discipline
\citep{miller2006}, so a condition is a set of grants a kernel checks rather than a
convention an agent could ignore. Where an answer key cannot reach, blind pairwise
comparison with a cross-family judge is the standard remedy \citep{zheng2023}, since
evaluators favour their own generations \citep{panickssery2024}; we preregistered such a
protocol but did not run it, and no result here depends on a judge.
