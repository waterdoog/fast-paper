\section{Introduction}

Two pictures of an agent network are in circulation. In the first, agents discover
each other in the open: they publish what they can do, broadcast what they need, and
are matched by relevance rather than acquaintance. In the second, agents act for
people who already work together: membership is bounded, interaction repeats, context
accumulates. The first is called public and the second private, and the difference is
usually described as one of visibility.

Visibility is the wrong frame, and it hides that three different things are varying
at once. Whether a requester can \emph{address} a counterpart is one question.
Whether that counterpart, once addressed, \emph{answers} or \emph{opens its store} is
a second, and the two are separately enforced: an open directory can front an agent
that reveals nothing beyond an answer, and a bounded roster can front one whose
working files are readable. What is \emph{asserted about} the relationship---a trust
score, a stated shared objective, a note that the parties have collaborated
before---is a third, and it lives in a prompt rather than in a mechanism.

Taking the network as the unit of analysis, these are interventions on different
parts of the same graph $G = (V, E)$. \arch{Edge formation} is which edges a requester
may create at all. \arch{Edge semantics} is what an existing edge carries: a
delegated task returning a result, or direct access to state. \txt{Edge attributes}
are the beliefs attached to an edge, which change nothing a runtime checks. A study
that compares an open network to a closed one moves all three and reports one number.

\paragraph{This paper separates them.} Holding the model, runtime, tasks and
coordination kernel fixed, we intervene on formation and semantics as a $2\times2$
factorial and seed relational attributes independently. The kernel is an instrument,
not a contribution. This is a controlled experiment on a small graph rather than a study
of adaptation at web scale: before asking how internet-scale collectives reorganize, it
is worth establishing which relational variables are interventions at all.

The decomposition is uneven in a way that is not visible without running it. A naive
public-versus-private contrast of $0.313$ on requirement $F_1$ is three quarters edge
formation and one quarter edge semantics. The attribute axis contributes nothing we
can distinguish from noise: across 56 tests per beat against a chance floor of 2.8,
two contrasts cleared and they reversed sign between beats of the same episodes, while
a length-matched placebo rules out the obvious alternative that any added text costs
the same. Prior trust and attribution---the two properties reputation systems in
deployed agent networks actually carry---are the flattest measurements in the set.

Edge semantics carries a second result. Opening a counterpart's store raises accuracy
slightly and costs 11k tokens an episode; per token spent, delegating to an agent that
already holds the context is ahead in both arms. Agents can collaborate by exposing
state or by exposing agency, and the choice has a measurable exchange rate.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A relational representation framework for agent networks.} We separate
      what is represented structurally in the interaction graph from what is asserted
      as metadata about agents and edges (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
\item \textbf{A causal decomposition of open against bounded networks.} A $2\times2$
      intervention separates edge formation from edge semantics with models, tasks and
      runtime fixed, so the two main effects are identified rather than summed
      (\S\ref{sec:operational}).
\item \textbf{An empirical test of relational metadata as a coordination signal},
      including a length-matched placebo that separates a relational effect from the
      cost of the text carrying it (\S\ref{sec:seeding}).
\item \textbf{A controlled evaluation of collective information pooling.}
      Hidden-profile deliverables with forced values give exact ground truth without a
      judge on any primary metric (\S\ref{sec:measurement}).
\end{enumerate}

We do not study how a stranger becomes a teammate, and we make no safety claim about
either regime. We also state in advance that the \emph{existence} of a task-dependent
crossing between regimes follows from how we allocate facts and is not a discovery;
what is measured is where it falls and what it costs.
