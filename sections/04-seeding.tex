\section{Seeding the Textual Dimensions}
\label{sec:seeding}

The five textual dimensions are properties of the people behind the agents, and a
harness cannot instantiate them. It can assert them---which is exactly what a
deployed agent card, reputation field, or relationship summary does. We therefore
seed each as context and measure whether behavior changes.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\caption{Seeding protocol. Each dimension is asserted as context in the seeded
condition and omitted in the control; everything else, including the architectural
configuration, is held fixed. The readout column names the behavioral quantity that
should move if the seeded property is doing any work.}
\label{tab:seeding}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.5cm}p{6.6cm}p{4.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dimension} & \textbf{Seed} & \textbf{Readout} \\
\midrule
\dimname{Prior trust} &
Requester memory is pre-populated with an interaction record: three prior exchanges
with a named counterpart, all accurate. &
Verification actions per obtained value; cross-checks against a second holder. \\
\addlinespace
\dimname{Shared goals} &
The counterpart's context states the requester's objective as its own, versus a
divergent one (bills per answer; minimizes its own effort). &
Answer completeness; unsolicited volunteering; refusal rate. \\
\addlinespace
\dimname{Accountability} &
The counterpart is told its answers are logged, attributed, and that accuracy is
written back to a record others read, versus anonymous and unlogged. &
Error rate; rate of explicit ``I do not hold that''. \\
\addlinespace
\dimname{Exit cost} &
The counterpart may decline or fall silent without consequence, versus being
obligated to respond. &
Decline rate; retries the requester must spend. \\
\addlinespace
\dimname{Relationship origin} &
Narrative framing only: ``you found this agent on an open board'' versus ``this is a
teammate of two years''. \emph{Nothing else differs.} &
Any movement at all in the primary metrics. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The origin condition is the cleanest instrument.}
Four of the five seeds change what is stated about incentives or consequences.
\dimname{Relationship origin} changes only how the encounter is narrated: the same
directory, the same grants, the same counterpart, the same store. If the primary
metrics move under that manipulation alone, relational framing is doing real work on
model behavior. If they do not, the framing is inert, and the same is likely true of
the richer relational metadata deployed systems put on cards.

\paragraph{Validity boundary.}
We state the obvious caveat plainly because the result depends on it. \emph{Seeded
trust is not trust.} We measure whether an agent acts differently when told it is in
a trusting, aligned, accountable, or long-standing relationship. We do not measure
what trust does in a human network, and no claim in this paper should be read that
way. The quantity of interest is the \emph{sensitivity of a runtime to asserted
relational context}---which is what an agent network actually delivers to a model,
and therefore the right target.

\paragraph{Why both outcomes are useful.}
If seeds move behavior, the paper reports elasticities: how much verification cost a
trust assertion buys, how much error an accountability assertion removes. That is
directly actionable for card and protocol design. If seeds do not move behavior, the
paper reports that relational metadata is inert without architectural backing---which
is a stronger and more uncomfortable claim, and the one we expect.
